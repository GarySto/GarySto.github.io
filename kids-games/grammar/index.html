Grammar and Punctuation by Year Group

This is what children in England learn about grammar and punctuation at primary school, one page for each year group, written in plain words with examples. It comes from the national curriculum (English Appendix 2), and every page has a printable version and easy ways to practise at home.

My eldest is in Year 4. When her teachers asked for punctuation practice, I made Punctuation Archery: pull back your bow and shoot the balloon with the right full stop, comma, apostrophe or speech mark. It's free, with no adverts and no sign-up.

When is each punctuation mark taught?

  • Capital letters and full stops: Year 1
  • Question marks and exclamation marks: Year 1
  • Commas in a list: Year 2
  • Apostrophes for missing letters (don't): Year 2
  • Apostrophes for one owner (Sam's): Year 2
  • Speech marks (inverted commas): Year 3
  • Full speech punctuation: Year 4
  • Apostrophes for more than one owner (the girls'): Year 4
  • Commas after fronted adverbials: Year 4
  • Brackets and dashes for extra information: Year 5
  • Semi-colons and colons: Year 6
  • Hyphens: Year 6

Each year builds on the one before, so children keep using everything they've already learned.

What these pages are, and aren't

They put English Appendix 2: vocabulary, grammar and punctuation into everyday language for parents. Schools choose their own order and some teach things earlier, so your child's teacher is always the best guide.
