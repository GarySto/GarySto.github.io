Digital marketing and AI: a future built on collaboration

1 November 2025 · Updated 12 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

These are questions about AI and digital marketing that I think are worth answering, so I've put my answers in one place.

What first drew you to digital marketing?

I spent much of my early career trying to work out where I fitted. After my early twenties in the Alps and on French rivers, I studied business with the Open University, and it was the marketing module that hooked me, particularly digital marketing, because it was driven by technology and data and it never stopped changing.

I joined Thomson Holidays, later TUI, in 2016, originally applying for the social media team before being asked to go for the PPC role instead. I've been in performance marketing ever since.

How has the industry changed since you started?

The goalposts have never stopped moving, from the ad formats themselves to how accounts are run day to day. I've written that history up in the speed of change in search driven by AI, and that constant change is part of what makes the industry exciting.

What excites you most right now?

AI, without a doubt, because for years one of the biggest weaknesses in marketing was the human factor in repetitive work: people get ill, take holidays or just get distracted. Having tools that keep the repetitive work efficient and on course is fascinating, and I've built some of them myself for my own projects.

Will AI replace human marketers?

No, AI will enhance what we do rather than replace us. Gone are the days of hiring someone to spend two days a week on search query reports, because AI makes that far quicker. But someone still needs to set the goals, check the output and step in when it goes wrong, and I've seen enough confident wrong answers from AI to know that check matters. I wrote more on this in will AI replace PPC managers?

Where does human judgement still matter?

In everything that never gets written down. Clients often hold back their real conversion data from the platform, and decisions about budget or strategy get made in boardrooms that never reach the people running the account. AI can analyse patterns, but it doesn't know what the client didn't say. Understanding people, culture and context is still a human job.

What's the biggest opportunity for businesses?

Time, mostly, because repetitive jobs like rewriting descriptions, uploading changes and fixing errors eat hours every week, and handing them to AI frees people to think. Reporting is the other area I see moving fastest, because pulling a weekly market picture together no longer has to swallow a whole afternoon.

What challenges do companies face adopting AI?

Many don't have a clear AI strategy. My personal concern is leadership treating AI purely as a cost-saving exercise while the people lower down fear being replaced, a dynamic that benefits nobody. Adoption is a change problem as much as a technology one.

One piece of advice for the next five years?

Treat AI as a tool to learn, not a threat to fight. The landscape has already changed and more change is coming, and the people who do best will be the ones who learn to use it well, early, and keep checking what it gives them.

How do you see AI shaping your role, as a help, a threat, or something in between? Tell me, I'd like to hear it.
