Start Small: The Mistakes I Made Building AI Tools

8 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

I have made plenty of mistakes building AI tools, at work and at home. I have spent hours, even weeks, building automated systems that turned out to be over complex, or that were made redundant in an instant when new tech came out. I would not call any of it wasted, because every one taught me something, but I would approach things differently if I were starting today.

Mistake One: Building Too Big, Too Early

The systems that cost me the most time were the ones I tried to make clever from day one. The more moving parts something has, the more there is to break, and the more painful it is when a new tool arrives that does the whole thing better.

My advice now is to look at the problem you actually want to solve first, then start small and build up. Do not try to get it perfect out of the gate. Get it working, with guardrails in place, and add to it once the simple version proves it is worth the effort.

Mistake Two: Trusting the AI Too Much

At home I have chased problems that did not exist, simply because the AI hallucinated or forgot the rules and instructions I had given it. Some conversations have been genuinely frustrating to troubleshoot in. When that happens, it helps to:

  • take a breath and go for a walk
  • come back with a different approach
  • ask the question slightly differently
  • start a brand new chat with just the key facts

I also now finish important work with an independent audit, asking the AI to check its own output against the facts and the original request.

Mistake Three: Not Protecting the Work

The most painful lesson is losing work that was working. These days I:

  • document every checkpoint
  • protect against crashes and mistakes
  • keep older files I can fall back on or use as a reference

It sounds boring, but it is the difference between a bad evening and a lost week.

Mistake Four: Making It Expensive

Early on it is tempting to throw paid tools at every problem. These days I work to strict rules: I keep things as low cost as possible, ideally free, and I spread my work across different AI tools so I am splitting the tokens and the costs rather than leaning on one. That keeps the projects sustainable, and it proves that anyone can do this, not just people with a big budget.

What I Would Tell a Marketer Starting Today

Start with one real problem, keep the first version small, put guardrails around it, and write down what you did as you go. Expect things to break and treat each break as something to learn from. The tools are changing so fast that the skill worth building is not any single system, it is knowing how to build, check and rebuild quickly.
