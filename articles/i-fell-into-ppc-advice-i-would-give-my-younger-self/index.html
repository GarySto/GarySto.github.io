I Fell Into PPC: Advice I Would Give My Younger Self

6 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

I accidentally fell into PPC. I was working as a generalist digital marketer, and I applied to join the social team at Thomson holidays, which later rebranded to TUI. During my application I was asked if I wanted to apply for the PPC role instead. I had built up some experience as a generalist, and apparently nobody was applying for the role at the time, so I decided to put my hat in the ring.

Luck, and Improving the Odds

I wish I could say it was something more planned than that, but it really was luck. Being honest, though, I was getting no luck with my CV and my job hunt until I completed my foundation degree with the Open University. After that I started getting more opportunities to interview.

That is the advice I would give my younger self. You cannot guarantee a win, but you can improve your odds. Doing the education, saying yes, and completing as many courses as I could really helped me get on and climb the ladder.

Not Always Lucky

I have not always been lucky, and the wind has not always blown in my direction. But I have given myself the best chance I could from a poor starting position. I have now got more than ten years of experience in performance marketing and PPC, I have built PPC accounts from the ground up, and I have managed teams looking after paid social, PPC, analytics and SEO.

Still Breaking Barriers

I am still trying to break barriers now. I am not an engineer and I have no formal education in it, but look at what I have built with AI so far, and I am only just starting. I built my own local AI assistant, an automation team around a demo trading account, and an AI team to help run this website.

What I Would Tell Someone Starting in PPC Today

The job is changing fast. When I started, a team of ten PPC people could spend their days doing things by hand, and today that can be a team of two. The skills that matter most now are knowledge, experience and the why: being able to spot the flaw in what a tool recommends, knowing how to fix it, and understanding the client. So say yes to the opportunities that come your way, keep learning, and get your hands on the AI tools early, because the people who can guide them will run the team of two.
