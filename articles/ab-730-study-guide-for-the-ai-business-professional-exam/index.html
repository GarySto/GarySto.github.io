AB-730 Study Guide for the AI Business Professional Exam

28 November 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

On 5th November 2026 I passed the AB-730 exam and earned the Microsoft Certified AI Business Professional certification. It's verifiable online on my Microsoft Learn profile. This AB-730 study guide covers how I prepared for the AI Business Professional exam, what helped most and what I'd tell a friend before they book it.

I should say up front that I'm not naturally good at tests. My ADHD makes sitting still for long stretches hard, and my eyesight means reading a screen for a long time can be tiring.

The resources I actually used

The first resource was this video, which I listened to on repeat. The second was the Course Preview playlist for AB-730, Transform business workflows with generative AI, on YouTube.

The third was the practice exams on Microsoft Learn. I also read through the Learn site itself. If you're starting out, Microsoft's official AB-730 study guide lists the topics the exam covers, so it's a good first stop. Practice exams are worth doing early, because they show you how the questions are worded before the real thing does.

Fit the studying into the day you already have

I did most of my revision while commuting and in the evenings. On one occasion I lay in bed with my headphones on and listened to a session that way. A short session you complete beats a long one you abandon.

Link the exam to your working knowledge

This is the part I'd stress most. I use Copilot every day, so a lot of the exam concepts were things I already knew in practice. What I had to do was refine my knowledge. That meant learning the correct names and the official terminology, and linking the principles in the course material to what each question was really asking. I'm all about linking my working knowledge with exam questions and concepts, and it worked for me.

If you have never used Copilot, stop

If you have never used Copilot and you're thinking of taking this exam, I'd tell you to pause. The exam is about advising people on how to use AI in their work, and jumping into it without having used AI yourself isn't the right way round.

Use the tools on real tasks first. After that, the course will start to feel like a description of things you have done rather than a list of facts to memorise. My broader thoughts on learning AI by doing it are in my piece on practical AI training in the UK.

The hard part is finding material you can take in

I didn't find any part of the content dull. The real challenge was finding learning resources that worked for me personally. Some websites had text that was small, long and dry. Some of the commentary in videos was hard to follow or understand.

Finding the format you can actually take on board is a big part of the preparation, so don't force yourself through material that doesn't land.

Sitting the exam remotely

I sat mine remotely, so my advice is to leave yourself enough time and prepare for things to go wrong, such as your internet connection or the software on your computer. Exams can be intimidating, and there's nothing wrong with feeling that. We aren't all built the same.

Once I was in, the exam itself was straightforward. The text comes up on the screen and you read and answer, which felt much like the practice exams. As long as you don't cheat and have no prohibited items, there's nothing to worry about.

The question that caught me out

One question did catch me off guard. It asked about Azure SQL and how it linked into the database, or whether it linked into Copilot. If I'm honest I had forgotten about it until now, and I haven't looked it up to see whether it was a trick answer.

I don't have a neat story about the moment a concept finally clicked. I have no idea when that happened, and I'm not going to pretend there was a single moment.

How it felt, and what comes next

When I saw the pass result on the screen I was buzzing. My wife was the first person I told, and I texted her the moment I got the result. I felt I had achieved something and that I had done it myself, so I was happy and proud. It was a big milestone for someone who finds exams hard, and it has encouraged me to go after more.

Two weeks later, on 19th November 2026, I passed the AB-731 as well and earned the Microsoft Certified AI Transformation Leader certification. I've set myself a challenge to complete as many AI certifications and courses as I can, to show that I know my stuff and to check my knowledge is where it should be. I want my career to move towards AI consulting and teaching, and certifications are one way of backing up what I say. I'll write up how I prepared for that one separately.

A simple plan if you are starting now

Start using Copilot on real tasks every day. Pick one video and one playlist and listen on repeat. Take a Microsoft Learn practice exam early, and note every term you didn't recognise. Then go back and match each one to something you have done in real life. Book the exam with spare time around it, and prepare for things to go wrong with your connection and computer.

You don't need to be great at exams to pass this one. You need hands-on knowledge, a format of learning that suits you and enough time to stay calm on the day.
