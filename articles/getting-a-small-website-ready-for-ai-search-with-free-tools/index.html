Getting a Small Website Ready for AI Search With Free Tools

6 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

When I started garystow.co.uk, I wanted to take everything I had learned in years of digital marketing and test it on a site of my own. I followed the latest news, signed up to everything I could find, and set out to use the tools properly rather than just read about them. I kept one rule all the way through, which is that everything has to be free, because I want to show that anyone can do what I do on a fair and level playing field.

On the technical side I worked through the basics that help search engines and AI systems understand a site. That meant a sitemap, a robots.txt file, IndexNow so new pages get noticed straight away, an llms.txt file and structured data on every page.

An AI team that does the work

The part I'm most proud of is how the work gets done. I used my analytics and SEO knowledge to build a team of AI automations, each with its own job, all working towards the goals I set for the site. One of them pulls the data from all those free tools, others look at SEO, technical health and content, and a supervisor works out what actually matters so that only the decisions come to me.

The biggest surprise so far has been how much stress that takes off. Knowing that proper teams are doing the heavy lifting means I can spend my time on the content and the improvements, which is where I'm actually needed.

What the numbers actually show

After a couple of months the honest picture is that it's early days. Over the last four weeks of data, Google showed the site in search results 143 times, which led to 3 clicks, and the site had around 360 page views in total. My guide to building your own local AI assistant is the page Google shows most, at an average position around ninth, and the cartoon village where visitors can watch my automations working has become the second most visited page after the home page. Most of my clicks come from people clicking through directly from LinkedIn and Reddit, because I'm not known well enough yet for generic search traffic.

Indexing is the real bottleneck, because Google has indexed only 22 of my 39 pages so far, while Bing has been quicker. For a new site with very few links pointing to it that's normal, and it's why I send a handful of pages to Google for indexing every day.

What Clarity shows about AI bots

The most eye-opening view for me has been the AI bot activity in Clarity. On 5th November 2026, with Cloudflare connected as the provider, the last seven days showed 12.87k requests in total, of which 77.73% (10.00k) were likely human traffic, while bots made 2,866 requests, which is 22.27% of all traffic. Google made up the largest share of those bots at 36.57%, with Microsoft at 6.52% and OpenAI at 3.38%, and the most requested path was robots.txt.

What struck me is that a site this small is already being crawled by that many sources, even though crawling is not the same thing as citing. My Local AI topic still shows zero citations, which doesn't surprise me because plenty of people set up local models well before I did and their pages are far more established. My modest goal is simply to be cited once, and I know the way to get there is to offer something different enough that an answer engine has a reason to point to me.

AI search doesn't reward a handful of pages. It rewards a site that covers its subject properly, with real detail and real experience, and that's where I'm putting my effort now, with fewer thin pages and more depth on the things I actually know.

What I would do first if you're starting today

I'd get the technical basics in place, submit your best pages for indexing every day, and keep asking what the data means and how to make it better. Patience is the part nobody can automate, because a new site with few links simply takes time.
