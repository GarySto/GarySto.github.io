I Fell Into PPC: Advice I Would Give My Younger Self

6 October 2026 · Updated 12 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

I didn't plan a career in paid search. I grew up in Gibraltar, moved to the UK in the early 2000s, and soon after that I left again to see the world.

Snow in winter, rivers in summer

I spent much of my early twenties in the European Alps, snowboarding and working in hotels through the winters. In the summers I worked as a canoe instructor on a river in France. I'm a fully qualified snowboard instructor.

Those years made me who I am and shaped the way I think. But as the years passed, I realised I had no money and no formal education, and I needed a proper job.

The degree that opened the door

Back in the UK, I started a business degree with the Open University. It was expensive, and I couldn't afford to finish it, but I completed two of the three years, which earned me a foundation degree. Before that, I was getting nowhere with my CV. After it, the interviews started coming.

The marketing module was what sparked my interest in digital marketing. But the most useful thing the course gave me wasn't marketing at all. It taught me how to write: how to write an executive summary, how to communicate clearly. I still use that every day.

How I fell into PPC

In 2016 I applied to join the social media team at Thomson Holidays, which later rebranded to TUI. During the process, I was asked if I'd go for the PPC role instead. Apparently nobody had applied, and they thought I'd be a good fit. So I put my hat in the ring.

It wasn't a small first job, either. Thomson sold package holidays, the budgets were in the millions, and I also looked after First Choice and Falcon (the Irish version of Thomson).

My first big mistake

Early on, I set a budget with the wrong figure. Everything ran out of money early in the day and stopped serving. It was spotted quickly, so the damage wasn't too bad, but it taught me lessons I still use: check the numbers before you press go, and never assume a change did what you meant it to.

How I actually learned paid search

The degree didn't teach me PPC, the free stuff did. Microsoft and Google both offered free online certifications, and I worked through them, collecting the badges. That's where I really built my knowledge. If you're starting out with no budget for training, that route is still there.

Climbing the ladder

From there I moved through agency and travel roles, all of which are on my LinkedIn. I built PPC accounts from the ground up, personally managed budgets of over £20m a year when I was client side, and ended up in a head of performance role managing teams across PPC, paid social, analytics and SEO.

Travel also taught me how quickly a plan can vanish, because the pandemic wiped out a year of plans almost overnight while the business still had to keep trading. I wrote more about that period in why change feels so hard.

What I'd tell my younger self

I wish I could say it was all planned. Some of it was luck. But luck wasn't all of it. You can't guarantee a win, but you can improve your odds. Doing the education, saying yes when an opportunity comes up, and completing every course you can get your hands on: that's what helped me climb from a poor starting position.

I haven't always been lucky, and the wind hasn't always blown my way. But I gave myself the best chance I could.

What I'd tell someone starting in PPC today

The basics are the same as when I started, because you're still buying traffic that has to turn into customers for a real business, but the tools around it keep moving. Bidding was automated years ago, and now AI is taking over much of the repetitive admin as well.

So learn the fundamentals properly, because you need them to check what the tools tell you. Get your hands on the AI tools early. And say yes to the odd opportunity, because that's how I got here.

I'm still trying to break barriers now. I'm not an engineer and I have no formal education in it, but I've built my own local AI assistant and automation teams that help run this website. I wrote about that side of things in how to learn AI as a marketer with no coding background, and more of my story is in how type 1 diabetes shaped my PPC career.
