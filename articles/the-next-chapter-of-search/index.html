The Next Chapter of Search

Advertising in a keywordless, agentic, websiteless world.

14 May 2026 · Gary Stow, Senior Client Solutions Manager, Financial Services, Microsoft Advertising / Microsoft AI

A familiar feeling, dressed up in new clothes

Every few years our industry collectively decides that search advertising, as we know it, is finished. The keyword is dead. The funnel is dead. SEO is dead. And every few years it turns out search has simply done what it always does, which is quietly reinvent itself while everyone is arguing on LinkedIn.

This time, though, the change feels structurally different. We are not redesigning the ad unit, we are watching the unit itself move. The keyword is being abstracted away, websites are increasingly optional, and a growing share of consumer journeys now involve at least one AI agent doing the heavy lifting on the user's behalf. Three things are happening at once, and that is what makes this moment worth paying attention to.

The good news, and I think this is the part too many people are missing, is that advertising does not vanish in this world. It changes shape. The same way it changed shape when we moved from text ads to Shopping, from manual bidding to Smart Bidding, from desktop to mobile, and from intent to audience. AI is not the end of the story. It is the next chapter.

We have been through bigger shifts than this

Paid search is older than most people remember. GoTo.com, later renamed Overture, launched the first commercial pay-per-click keyword auction back in 1998. Google followed with AdWords on 23 October 2000, originally on a CPM model, before pivoting to PPC in 2002. Quality Score, the relevance-weighted auction mechanic that most practitioners now associate with the early days of AdWords, did not actually arrive until August 2005. Yahoo bought Overture in 2003, Google ate everyone else's lunch, and the modern industry was born.

In the twenty-seven years since, we have lived through expanded text ads, responsive search ads, Performance Max, audience signals, the slow death of exact match, the resurrection of broad match, and approximately one thousand renamings of essentially the same feature. The lesson from that history is not that PPC is fragile. It is that PPC is unusually good at swallowing new technology and carrying on.

If you have been in this industry long enough to remember manually adjusting CPCs in Excel at midnight, you have already lived through three or four moments where everyone was certain the game was about to end. It did not. It just got more interesting.

Three forces reshaping the surface of search

1. The keyword is being demoted to a signal. On 6 May 2025 Google launched AI Max for Search campaigns, with open beta on 27 May. It bundles broad match, keywordless matching, AI-generated headlines and Final URL expansion into a single toggle. Google's own figures suggest roughly a 14% conversion lift at similar CPA on average, rising to 27% on accounts still leaning hard on exact and phrase match. Those are vendor-supplied numbers, so treat them as direction of travel rather than gospel, but the direction is unmistakable. The keyword is no longer the unit you buy. It is one of many signals the system uses to decide whether your ad is the right answer for a moment.

Search Engine Land's Anu Adegbola put it neatly: Google wants advertisers to move beyond traditional keywords, and AI Max is the new on-ramp to that goal. The on-ramp is being widened on purpose.

2. Agents are turning answers into actions. In the space of eighteen months, the plumbing for agentic commerce went from concept to standard. Anthropic open-sourced the Model Context Protocol on 25 November 2024. OpenAI launched Operator on 23 January 2025. Google announced Agent2Agent on 9 April 2025 with fifty partners including Salesforce, SAP, PayPal and Workday, then donated it to the Linux Foundation in June 2025, where it now carries more than a hundred supporting organisations.

Translated out of acronyms, agents can now talk to other agents, and they can talk to tools. A consumer-side assistant can call a brand-side agent, which can call a stock system, which can place an order, all in one conversation. The practical implication for advertisers is that the messy middle, the part of the journey where consumers used to compare, browse and click through a dozen tabs, is increasingly being handed to agents. Brands that are not legible to those agents will not be considered at all.

3. Half of all searches no longer leave Google. SparkToro and Datos run the most rigorous open data on this, and their Q4 2025 reading is striking. 56% of US desktop Google searches are now zero-click. Looking at SparkToro's 2024 study, only 374 of every 1,000 US searches reach the open web, with the EU figure slightly lower at 360 per 1,000. Google itself now drives clicks for fewer than half of all searches, down from more than 60% a decade ago.

AI tool traffic across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity grew about 50% year-on-year to roughly 0.77% of all US desktop activity by December 2025. Smaller than the headlines suggest, but the more interesting finding from Rand Fishkin is that AI traffic appears to be additive, not substitutive. Traditional search is still growing alongside it. Users are not abandoning search, they are using more of it, in more places.

UK lens: where the British market actually sits

Global numbers do a lot of the heavy lifting in this debate, but the UK picture is sharper than people assume. A few anchor points worth keeping in mind.

  • A £46.7bn market, still search-led. UK advertising investment reached £46.7 billion in 2025, up 6.4% year-on-year (AA/WARC Expenditure Report, April 2026). Search still accounts for the largest single share at 38.3% on the refreshed AA/WARC channel definitions, which now report search separately from retail media.
  • Paid search alone is a £16.6bn line. IAB UK's Digital Adspend report (April 2025) put UK paid search at £16.6 billion in 2024, 47% of all UK digital ad spend, growing 13% year-on-year inside a £35.53 billion UK digital market.
  • British consumers have adopted AI search faster than the chatter suggests. Ofcom's Online Nation 2025 recorded 1.8 billion UK visits to ChatGPT in the first eight months of 2025, up from 368 million in the same window of 2024. That is roughly a fivefold rise in a year.
  • AI Overviews are now a normal feature of UK results. The same Ofcom report finds that around 30% of UK Google searches now display an AI Overview, and 53% of UK adults say they see these AI-generated summaries often, typically without having sought them out.
  • Generative AI is mainstream, not niche. By mid-2024, 41% of UK internet users aged 16+ had used a generative AI tool in the past year (Ofcom Online Nation 2024). ChatGPT had been used by 33% of all UK internet users, with Microsoft Copilot the second most-used tool.
  • The regulator is already paying attention. The CMA's AI Foundation Models update (April 2024) flagged three interlinked risks to fair, open and effective competition in this space, including the risk that incumbent platforms exploit positions in consumer-facing markets to distort choice in AI foundation-model services and their downstream deployment. The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act has since added teeth to that view.

Sources: AA/WARC Expenditure Report (April 2026); IAB UK Digital Adspend (April 2025); Ofcom Online Nation 2024 and 2025; CMA AI Foundation Models update (April 2024).

Everyone is trying everything: a tour of the LLM monetisation race

If you want to know how an industry feels about its future, watch how it tries to fund it. Right now, the LLM space looks like a food court at lunchtime. Every stall is testing a different recipe, hoping to find the one customers will queue for.

OpenAI: from "last resort" to ad-supported flagship. Sam Altman called advertising a last resort and uniquely unsettling in 2024. By September 2025, ChatGPT had launched Instant Checkout with Etsy and over a million Shopify merchants, powered by the open-source Agentic Commerce Protocol co-built with Stripe. In January 2026 OpenAI announced ad pilots; the Free and Go tiers got banners and sponsored shopping cards in February 2026, with rollout reaching the UK in early May 2026. Separately, on 6 October 2025 at DevDay, OpenAI launched Apps in ChatGPT, a distinct product that lets third parties such as Booking.com, Expedia and Zillow run inside the chat surface. If you ever wondered how quickly a stated principle could give way to a business model, the answer is roughly eighteen months.

Perplexity: first to ship, now browser-first. Perplexity launched Sponsored Questions on 12 November 2024 with Whole Foods, Indeed, Universal McCann and PMG. The Comet browser moved from preview in July 2025 into general availability on 1 October 2025, accompanied by Comet Plus, a $5 a month subscription that shares 80% of revenue back to publishers. Perplexity CEO Aravind Srinivas has argued that agents that know a user well can serve ads that are genuinely useful rather than intrusive. Whether users feel the same way is the open question. The ad pipeline has been launched, paused, relaunched and paused again across 2025-26, which tells you more about the difficulty than the press releases do.

Google: ads where the eyeballs still go. Ads inside AI Overviews have run in US English since May 2025 and now appear across roughly twelve countries, with testing inside the new AI Mode interface. Gemini, the chatbot, is officially ad-free, but Search and Ads lead Nick Fox told WIRED last year that they're not ruling them out, which is industry-speak for give us a quarter or two. The bigger bet, in my view, is the Agent Payments Protocol announced on 16 September 2025 with sixty partners including Mastercard, Amex, PayPal, Coinbase, Shopify and Adyen. If agents become the layer that completes transactions, the company that controls the rails for those transactions captures the take-rate. That is a search business, just not one that looks like the ten blue links.

Microsoft: embedded into Copilot. Microsoft Advertising shipped Sponsored Suggested Responses and Dynamic Filters in Copilot in March 2025, and refocused its supply-side offering around Microsoft Monetize in spring 2025 following the announced wind-down of the Invest DSP on 14 May 2025. Monetize lets publishers run their own ads inside their experiences. The strategy is closer to enable everyone than capture everyone, and it sits naturally next to the rest of the productivity stack.

Anthropic: the deliberate hold-out. On 4 February 2026 Anthropic publicly committed to keeping Claude permanently ad-free, arguing that a conversation with Claude is not a good place for advertising. They then ran Super Bowl LX ads making the same point. The strategy rests on enterprise API revenue and consumer subscriptions, a bet that a meaningful slice of users will pay to avoid advertising entirely. Whether that slice is one percent or twenty percent of the market will decide the next five years.

Meta, Amazon, X. From 16 December 2025, Meta began using AI chat content to target ads across Facebook and Instagram. Amazon launched Sponsored Ads inside Rufus in early 2025, with reports in May 2026 suggesting the brand is being folded into a wider Alexa shopping agent. X, using xAI's Grok, launched Prefill with Grok on 25 February 2025, letting advertisers pre-seed the prompts users send to Grok about their brand, which is either ingenious or alarming depending on which side of the desk you sit on.

Five monetisation models shaping the next two years

Across all that activity, five recurring models stand out. None of them are settled, all of them are funded.

  • Sponsored answers and sponsored citations. Native ad units inside the generated response itself. Perplexity Sponsored Questions, Microsoft Sponsored Suggested Responses, OpenAI's banner-below-answer format. The new above the fold is inside the paragraph.
  • Agentic commerce with transaction fees. Take a cut of the completed action rather than the click. OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol with Stripe, and Google's Agent Payments Protocol with sixty partners, are the architecture for this. It is the biggest single shift to the search-revenue model since AdWords in 2000.
  • Hyper-personalised ads via assistant and browser context. Meta using AI chat content, Perplexity using Comet browsing data. A step-change in targeting precision and, simultaneously, the biggest regulatory tripwire in the room, particularly in the UK and EU.
  • Publisher revenue-share. Perplexity Comet Plus shares 80% with publishers; Microsoft Monetize lets publishers run their own AI ads. A direct response to the "AI is killing referral traffic" complaint.
  • Answer Engine Optimisation and Generative Engine Optimisation. A whole new agency service line, getting brands cited inside AI answers rather than ranked on a results page. Dentsu, Publicis, WPP and Omnicom all built dedicated practices in 2025-26.

So where does advertising still play?

Take all of the above and the picture becomes clearer. The places advertising will live in the next five years are not mysterious, they are just different.

  • Inside the answer. Where the eyeballs go, ads follow. The question is not if but in what format, how clearly marked, and how trusted.
  • Inside the product feed. Agents do not read clever headlines, they parse structured data. The product feed is the new ad copy, and the brands that maintain rich, clean, machine-readable inventory will be the ones agents recommend.
  • Inside the transaction. When an agent completes a purchase on behalf of a user, the platform that brokered the transaction takes a fee. Performance media meets payments, with all the implications that has for attribution and measurement.
  • Inside the citation. Being mentioned by name inside an AI answer is the new top-of-funnel. It looks a lot like PR, behaves a lot like SEO, and is going to need a new measurement vocabulary.

Mike King at iPullRank calls this work Relevance Engineering, arguing in his January 2026 essay Moving from a Google-shaped Web to an Agent-shaped Web that traditional ranking factors explain very little of what drives citations in AI search. He may be early, but he is not wrong.

The clearest signal of all: who is buying ads

If you want to test how seriously to take this shift, look at where the money is being spent. According to CNBC and Sensor Tower, AI companies themselves spent more than $1 billion on digital advertising in 2025, up 126% year-on-year. The very companies arguing they will replace traditional search are also among the biggest buyers in it. The buyers are becoming the sellers, and the sellers have to keep buying.

On the demand side, VML's own Future Shopper 2025 survey found that 48% of shoppers are excited for AI-to-AI shopping and 52% would like their own shopping agent, vendor-supplied research so handle with the usual caveats. WPP launched WPP Open Pro on 23 October 2025, an agentic platform built on a Google partnership reportedly worth $400 million. CEO Cindy Rose OBE noted in the 2025 Annual Report that she is confident that in the era of AI, marketing's role will become more, not less, important as it continues to evolve and becomes more fragmented and complex. That is a polite way of saying the work is multiplying, not disappearing.

What this means for the next two years

PPC is twenty-seven years old this year. In that time it has survived the dotcom crash, mobile, social, programmatic, GDPR, the death of third-party cookies and at least one Google rebrand of essentially every feature it has ever shipped. It will survive AI. But survive is the wrong word. It will absorb AI, the same way it absorbed everything else.

Three habits will separate the operators who do well from the ones who keep complaining about Performance Max in 2028:

  • Operate AI Max with discipline. The conversion lift is real, the transparency is reduced. Brand exclusions, clean conversion data and structured testing become the craft.
  • Treat the product feed as the new ad copy. If the agent cannot parse it, the customer cannot buy it. Invest in structured data the same way you used to invest in landing pages.
  • Optimise for citation, not just click. Being mentioned inside an AI answer is the new awareness layer. Track it, measure it, and stop being surprised when a brand that does shows up everywhere and the one that doesn't, doesn't.

The keyword is not dead. It has been promoted out of the buying unit and demoted into a signal. The website is not dead. It is becoming one of several places a brand has to be legible. The funnel is not dead. It is being managed, increasingly, by agents acting on behalf of the user. None of that ends advertising. It just changes who the audience is, and what counts as a click.

If you joined this industry expecting it to stand still, you joined the wrong industry. If you joined it because it never does, this is the most interesting chapter we have written in years.
