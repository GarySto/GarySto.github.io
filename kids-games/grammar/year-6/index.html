Year 6 Grammar and Punctuation

Year 6 brings in the trickier marks, semi-colons, colons, dashes and hyphens, plus the passive voice and the difference between formal and informal writing.

Below is everything on the Year 6 list in the national curriculum for England, in plain words with examples, plus the words your child will hear in class and a few easy ways to practise at home.

Punctuation in Year 6

Semi-colons, colons and dashes between linked sentences

These can join two sentences that are closely linked.

  • It's raining; I'm fed up. (the curriculum's example)

Colons and semi-colons in lists

A colon introduces a list, and semi-colons can split up long items in it.

  • We packed three things: a tent; two warm sleeping bags; and a torch with spare batteries.

Bullet points

Punctuate a bullet list the same way all the way down.

Hyphens to stop confusion

A hyphen can change the meaning.

  • man-eating shark (a shark that eats people)
  • re-cover a chair (cover it again) and recover (get better)

Sentences and words in Year 6

The passive voice

The passive changes what the sentence focuses on.

  • The dog chased the ball. (active)
  • The ball was chased by the dog. (passive)

Formal and informal

Choosing words and structures to suit the writing, from question tags in chat to the subjunctive in very formal writing.

  • find out (informal) and discover (formal)
  • He's your friend, isn't he? (question tag)
  • If I were you… (subjunctive)

Synonyms and antonyms

Words that mean the same, and words that mean the opposite.

  • big and large (synonyms)
  • big and small (antonyms)

Linking ideas across a piece

Repeat a key word or use phrases like on the other hand, in contrast and as a result.

Layout

Headings, columns, bullets and tables help readers find their way.

Year 6 builds on everything from the years before, so children keep using it all. See Year 5 for what came just before.

Words your child will hear in Year 6

The curriculum lists these terms for children to understand and use in Year 6.

subjectWho or what does the verb: The dog chased the ball.
objectWho or what the verb is done to: The dog chased the ball.
activeThe subject does the action: The dog chased the ball.
passiveThe subject has the action done to it: The ball was chased by the dog.
synonymA word with the same or nearly the same meaning: big and large.
antonymA word with the opposite meaning: hot and cold.
ellipsisLeaving out words the reader can work out, or the three dots … that show a pause.
hyphenThe short - mark that joins words: well-known, man-eating.
colonThe : mark that introduces a list or an explanation.
semi-colonThe ; mark that joins two linked sentences or splits up a list of long items.
bullet pointsA list with a dot or mark at the start of each item.

Easy ways to practise at home

  • Turn sentences from a news story into the passive, then back again.
  • Say the same thing formally and informally (Can I have… / May I please…).
  • Collect hyphenated words you spot around the house.
  • Play Punctuation Archery (Mixed challenge, to keep Year 1 to 4 punctuation sharp): pull back the bow and shoot the balloon that finishes the sentence. Get it wrong and someone throws a tomato at you.
