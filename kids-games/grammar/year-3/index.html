Year 3 Grammar and Punctuation

Year 3 brings in speech marks (inverted commas) for the first time. Children also learn a or an, word families, paragraphs, and words that say when, where and why.

Below is everything on the Year 3 list in the national curriculum for England, in plain words with examples, plus the words your child will hear in class and a few easy ways to practise at home.

Punctuation in Year 3

Speech marks for direct speech

Put inverted commas around the exact words someone says. Children often remember them as 66 at the start and 99 at the end.

  • The teacher said, “Pens down.”

Sentences and words in Year 3

A or an

Use an before a vowel sound and a before a consonant sound.

  • a rock
  • an open box

Word families

Spotting words that share a root helps with meaning and spelling.

  • solve, solution, solver, dissolve

Prefixes

Prefixes like super-, anti- and auto- make new words.

  • supermarket
  • antisocial
  • autograph

When, where and why

Conjunctions (when, before, after, so, because), adverbs (then, next, soon) and prepositions (before, during, in) explain time, place and cause.

  • We went inside before the rain started.

Paragraphs and headings

Group ideas into paragraphs, and use headings and sub-headings in fact writing.

The present perfect

A verb form for something that happened and still matters now.

  • He has gone out to play. (not He went out to play)

Year 3 builds on everything from the years before, so children keep using it all. See Year 2 for what came just before.

Words your child will hear in Year 3

The curriculum lists these terms for children to understand and use in Year 3.

prepositionA word that shows where or when, before a noun: under the table, after lunch.
conjunctionA joining word: and, but, because, when, if.
word familyWords built from the same root: teach, teacher, teaching.
prefixLetters added to the start of a word: un + kind = unkind.
clauseA group of words with a verb in it: the dog barked.
subordinate clauseA clause that can't be a sentence on its own: because it was cold.
direct speechThe exact words someone says, written inside inverted commas.
consonantAny letter that isn't a vowel: b, c, d, f and so on.
vowelThe letters a, e, i, o and u.
inverted commasSpeech marks: the 66 and 99 shapes that go around spoken words.

Easy ways to practise at home

  • Read a comic together: speech bubbles are the words that go inside speech marks.
  • Pick a root word like play and see how many family members you can find.
  • Ask them to retell their day using then, after that and because.
  • Play Punctuation Archery (Speech marks): pull back the bow and shoot the balloon that finishes the sentence. Get it wrong and someone throws a tomato at you.
