Why I Run AI at Home, and LM Studio Tips for Beginners

6 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

I grew up poor, homeless poor. What might be £20 to one person can be a week's worth of food for another. I know my hardware is pretty good, but I really wanted to stay true to my principles, and running AI at home with no token cost is a big part of that. I want to show that anyone can do what I do, and that it is a fair, level playing field.

Control, Not Just Cost

Cost was only part of it. I also wanted control over my AI system. I wanted to build my own things and get a deeper understanding of what it takes to have a really good working AI system, rather than just renting one.

If I am honest, I also wanted to know whether I could do it at all. I often feel like the idiot in the room when people use big words, and I prefer the direct, simple approach. Building my own local AI, Wade, was a way of pushing myself to find out whether I was smart enough for this kind of thing.

What Wade Does

Wade runs on my own PC, and its job is to help manage my automated trading and my website. I built it using only free tools, and wrote up exactly how in how to actually build your own local AI assistant.

My Advice for Trying LM Studio for the First Time

LM Studio is how I run my local models, and if you are trying it for the first time, my advice is simple.

First, understand its limitations. A local model is not going to behave exactly like the big paid ones, and knowing that up front saves a lot of frustration. Second, ask your other AI for help. There is so much in LM Studio, but in reality you only need to use a few things, and the tools already out there can help you find what you want quickly. Third, start small and get one thing working before you try to build anything clever.

The Honest Bit

I love the fact there is no token cost, and I should probably use Wade more than I do. But I like Claude too much, so in practice I use both: Wade for the jobs it does well and cheaply at home, and Claude for the heavier coding and problem solving. Knowing which tool to use for which job is a skill in itself.
