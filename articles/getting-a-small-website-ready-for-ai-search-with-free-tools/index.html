Getting a Small Website Ready for AI Search With Free Tools

6 October 2026 · Updated 12 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

When I started garystow.co.uk, I wanted to take everything I'd learned in years of digital marketing and test it on a site of my own. I followed the latest news, signed up to everything I could, and set out to use the tools properly rather than just read about them, with one rule throughout: keep it free.

The free tools I set up

Everything I use is free:

  • Google Search Console, to see which searches the site appears for in Google
  • Bing Webmaster Tools, for the same view in Bing, which I treat as equal to Google
  • Microsoft Clarity, to see how people and AI bots actually use the pages
  • GA4, for traffic and engagement
  • Ahrefs' free plan, which runs a weekly audit of the whole site

I compared three of these in detail in Clarity vs GA4 vs Bing Webmaster Tools.

Making the site readable for AI

On the technical side I worked through the basics that help search engines and AI systems understand a site: a sitemap, robots.txt, IndexNow so new pages get noticed straight away, an llms.txt file and structured data on every page. I wrote the full list up in getting a website ready for AI search.

An AI team that does the work

The part I'm most proud of is how the work gets done. I've used my analytics and SEO knowledge to build a team of AI automations, each with its own job, working towards the site's goals. One pulls the data from all those free tools, others look at SEO, technical health and content, a supervisor works out what actually matters, and only the decisions come to me. They now fix basic things like page titles and descriptions on their own and tell me afterwards.

The biggest surprise so far has been how much stress that takes off. Knowing that proper teams are doing the heavy lifting means I can spend my time on the content and the improvements, which is where I'm actually needed.

What the numbers actually show

After a couple of months, the honest picture is that it's early days. Over the last four weeks of data, Google showed the site in search results 143 times, which led to 3 clicks, and the site had around 360 page views in total. My guide to building your own local AI assistant is the page Google shows most, at an average position around ninth. The cartoon village where visitors can watch my automations working has become the second most visited page after the home page.

Indexing is the real bottleneck, because Google has indexed only 22 of my 39 pages so far, while Bing has been quicker. For a new site with very few links pointing to it, that's normal, and it's why I send a handful of pages to Google for indexing every day.

The lesson so far

AI search doesn't reward a handful of pages, it rewards a site that covers its subject properly, with real detail and real experience, and that's where I'm putting my effort now: fewer thin pages and more depth on the things I actually know.

Why I'm doing it this way

Part of this is a principle, because a small business owner or a marketer starting out doesn't need an agency or expensive software to get their site ready for AI search. They need the free tools, some patience, and a willingness to keep asking what the data means and how to make it better.
