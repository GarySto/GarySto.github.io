Digital marketing and AI: a future built on collaboration

1 November 2025 · Gary Stow, Senior Client Solutions Manager, Financial Services, Microsoft Advertising / Microsoft AI

I was recently asked a set of questions about AI and digital marketing. Here's how I answered them.

What first drew you to digital marketing, and how has the industry evolved since you started?

I spent many of my early career years trying to find where I fit in. After starting a degree in business through the Open University, I realised marketing was what grabbed my attention, particularly digital marketing. It was technology-driven, data-based, and constantly evolving.

Fast forward to today, and the landscape looks very different. Standard text ads gave way to expanded text ads, then dynamic search ads, then responsive search ads, and now Performance Max. The goalposts kept moving, and that's what made it exciting.

When you think about the current state of digital marketing, what excites you the most?

AI. For years, the biggest weakness in marketing was the human factor in repetitive tasks. People get sick, take holidays, or simply get distracted. Finding a tool that keeps everything efficient and on course was always going to be fascinating. I'm fortunate to have a front-row seat as the landscape evolves, and to see how exciting many of the upcoming developments are.

AI is everywhere in marketing conversations. How do you see it shaping the future of campaign management and creativity?

AI will automate repetitive tasks, freeing marketers to focus on strategy and creativity. It can provide insights and data-driven recommendations, but the human touch is irreplaceable when it comes to understanding consumer emotions and storytelling. Behaviours change over time, with trends, and as people age. A human will always be needed to help machines navigate the randomness of human behaviour.

Do you think AI will replace human marketers, or will it enhance what we do?

No. AI will enhance our work, not replace us. My concern is that many brands haven't fully fleshed out their AI needs and approaches, so knee-jerk reactions won't serve them well. Gone are the days of hiring an intern to spend two days a week on search query reports. AI makes that significantly faster and more cost-efficient. But someone still needs to train the AI and step in when needed.

What role do you believe human intuition still plays in a world increasingly driven by automation and algorithms?

Human intuition is irreplaceable in understanding consumer emotions and cultural nuances. AI can analyse patterns, but it can't replicate empathy. The best campaigns blend data with creativity.

For businesses, what's the biggest opportunity AI brings to digital marketing today?

Efficiency and ROI. Think about how many hours a week are spent on repetitive tasks, rewriting meta descriptions, uploading changes, fixing errors. AI reduces mistakes and frees up time for humans to do what they do best: think.

What challenges do you see companies facing when adopting AI-driven tools?

Companies often struggle with a lack of AI strategy. My personal concern is leadership using AI purely as a cost-saving exercise, while those lower down fear replacement. That dynamic benefits no one.

If you had to give one piece of advice to marketers preparing for the next five years, what would it be?

Embrace AI as a tool, not a replacement. The technology is here, and the landscape has already changed. More change is coming. You can fight the tide, but those who win will be the ones who learn to use AI and get the best from it sooner.
