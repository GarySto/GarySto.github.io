A Friday Build Day That Showed How Fast AI Is Moving

8 October 2026 · Updated 12 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

On a Friday I was invited to a hands-on AI build day run by Microsoft Advertising with one of our agency partners, and I was billed as the AI expert in the room. It was a full-day event with around fifty people, split into teams, and it's stayed with me since because of how much it showed about the speed things are moving.

My role on the day

As the AI expert, my job was to offer advice and experience, give my thoughts on what people were building, and share where I think things are heading from a performance marketing automation point of view. I wasn't there to build, I was there to help the teams get further, faster.

How the day ran

The structure worked really well, starting with people demoing things they'd already built, then moving into the theory, and finishing with a hands-on workshop where teams built solutions for the business.

I loved hearing what others had built early in the day. Much of it was a fun flashback to my own journey, back when the only AI I had access to was at work, and I knew exactly where people would get stuck because I'd been stuck in the same places, working it out alone.

How fast this is moving

The thing that took me the best part of a month to build on my own was something a team could now get most of the way to in a single afternoon, with the right support in the room. That says a lot about how quickly the tools, and people's skills with them, are improving.

A few ideas stopped me in my tracks, properly new and nothing I'd seen before. Some I liked so much I had to try my hardest not to go home and recreate them, even though I don't really need them for my own work. That's been happening to me a lot lately, and reading what people share online, like someone who built a lab to test trading ideas against years of market data, or someone who turned a whole forum into a live dashboard, keeps adding to the list.

What I would change

If I'd had the day to myself, I'd have made the workshop a little longer with more of a build-up. The teams had a good amount of time, but it still felt short for ideas that good.

I'd also love to go back and see whether any of those ideas were developed further or adopted by the business, because a build day creates a lot of energy and the real test is what happens to it afterwards.

What makes a build day work

The order of the day is the part I'd copy. Starting with real things people had already built set the tone, because it showed everyone what was possible before anyone sat through theory. Ending hands-on meant people left having made something rather than just having heard about it. Getting people hands-on with the tools is where most AI adoption stalls, and a build day goes straight at that problem.

A quick detour into games

The day before, I'd been on a panel talking about AI and commerce, and somehow ended up at the stand afterwards chatting with the Xbox team about it. I wanted to work in games as a teenager, so that part of the week wasn't bad either.
