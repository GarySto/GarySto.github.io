A Friday Build Day That Showed How Fast AI Is Moving

8 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

On a Friday I was invited to a hands-on AI build day run by Microsoft Advertising with one of our agency partners, and I got billed as the AI expert in the room. I really liked the day. It started with people demoing things they had already built, then moved into the theory, and finished with a hands-on workshop where teams built solutions for the business.

Watching People Build

I loved hearing what others had built early in the day. Much of it was a fun flashback to my own journey, back when the only AI I had access to was at work. I knew exactly where they would get stuck, because I had been stuck in the same places myself, working it out alone.

The thing that took me the best part of a month to build by myself is now something a team can get most of the way to in a single afternoon, with the right support in the room. That says a lot about how fast this is moving.

A few ideas stopped me completely in my tracks, properly new and nothing I had seen before. Some of them I loved so much I had to try my hardest not to go home and recreate them, even though I do not really need them for my own work.

What I Would Change

If I had the day to myself, I think I would make the workshop a little longer, with a bit more of a build up to it. The teams had a good amount of time, but it still felt too short for ideas that good.

I would also love to go back and see whether any of the ideas from the day were developed further or adopted by the business. A build day sparks a lot of energy, and the real test is what happens to that energy afterwards.

What Makes a Build Day Work

The order of the day was the part I would copy. Starting with real things people had already built set the tone, because it showed everyone what was possible before anyone sat through theory. Ending hands on meant people left having made something, not just having heard about it. Getting people using the tools is, I think, one of the biggest challenges with AI, and a build day tackles it head on.

A Quick Detour Into Games

The day before, I had been on a panel talking about AI and commerce, and somehow ended up back at the stand chatting with the Xbox team about it afterwards. I wanted to work in games as a teenager, so that bit of the week was not bad either.

I wrote more about learning by building in how a colleague joked and dared me to build something, and about how I think teams can approach this in practical AI training in the UK.
