How to Get a Sceptical Colleague to Actually Try AI

7 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

If someone really does not want to try something, there is nothing you can do until they are ready, and trying to force them is not good for anyone. That is the honest starting point for anyone trying to get a sceptical colleague, or a sceptical boss, to give AI a go.

Start by Understanding the Scepticism

People are sceptical about AI for lots of different reasons. Some have tried it and got a bad answer. Some worry about what it means for their job. Some simply do not see what problem it would solve for them. Each of those needs a different conversation, so the first step is listening rather than selling.

Find One Simple Problem It Solves

The best way in is to look at the challenges that person already has and ask whether any of them have a simple AI solution. Not a grand transformation, just one small, real thing:

  • an email they dread writing
  • a report that eats an afternoon every week
  • notes from a meeting that never get written up
  • a pile of data they never have time to look through

One genuine win on their own work will do more than any demo of what AI can do for someone else.

Take Baby Steps

Helping people take simple baby steps is a great way to break down barriers, as long as they have a growth mindset. Each small success makes the next step easier, and before long they are finding uses you never thought of.

It also helps to be honest about the limits. AI can be confidently wrong, and it needs checking. Telling a sceptic that up front, and showing them how you check its work, builds far more trust than pretending it is perfect.

Give Them a Simple Way to Think About It

A lot of scepticism comes from not knowing what to expect. The way I explain AI is that it is like a graduate fresh out of university, with loads of great ideas and suggestions but no real world experience. The sceptic has that experience, and the AI needs it to be useful. Framed like that, trying AI stops being a threat and becomes something closer to mentoring, which most experienced people are happy to do.

Know When to Wait

If someone does not have that growth mindset yet, it is best to wait until they are ready. Pushing harder usually just hardens the resistance. Keep sharing small wins, keep the door open, and let curiosity do the work. In my experience the moment it clicks for someone is when they see it solve a problem they actually care about.

It Is Really About Change

Getting someone to try AI is really a change management problem, not a technology problem. I wrote more about that in why change feels so hard, and how we can make it easier, and about the bigger shift AI is driving in digital marketing and AI: a future built on collaboration.
