ADHD and AI Tools: How I Stop Things Slipping

6 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Depending on the type of ADHD you have, there can be a whole range of challenges to work around, so I can only speak for my own experience. For me it is getting distracted and being forgetful. It is very easy for an email to go unnoticed, or to say I am going to do something and then forget about it completely.

Reminders That Come to Me

Rather than relying on my memory, I have built solutions that email me throughout the day and the week. They prompt me when work has been missed or is still outstanding, and they help me prioritise my workload so I am not just reacting to whatever shouted loudest.

One small trick that works really well for me is getting the system to draft the email for me. Seeing a ready-made draft sitting there is a great reminder that I need to finish the task, and it removes the friction of starting from a blank page, which is often exactly where I get stuck.

Checks That Catch What I Miss

I have also built solutions that check my work, to make sure everything is accurate and completed on time. When your attention jumps around, it is easy to miss a detail or leave something half done. A system that looks back over the work does not replace judgement, but it catches a lot of what slips through.

The Human Side Still Matters

The tools help, but they are not the whole answer. I try to make sure I take breaks through the day, so I come back focused and ready to finish things. I also like to run, which does more for my head than any app.

My ADHD is part of why I struggled to keep focus when I first tried to learn Python. AI was different, because it caught my fascination, and building tools around my own brain rather than fighting it is one of the most useful things I have done with it.
