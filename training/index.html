02

How I run a session

I start by asking who it's for. For a business we look at business problems and needs, for a team it's whether they're trying to work better together, and for an individual it's what they want to learn. I also ask which AI the team uses, because that changes the whole session.

Sessions are hands-on. I'd rather run a lab day or a round table where people work on a real problem and pitch what they've built than talk through a deck. Getting people to use the tool first, then find its limits, is one of the biggest challenges in AI.

Mixed levels are normal, and I learned how to handle them as a snowboard instructor. A class can only go as fast as its slowest learner, so I'd give the stronger riders something harder, like riding in their opposite stance, while the others caught up. AI training works the same way, so I meet people where they are and keep everyone moving.

I use humour to relax a room and everyday comparisons to make AI relatable. The one I use most is the fresh graduate, who has loads of great ideas and no real-world experience, and needs yours to guide them.

How you'll know it worked

A month later I'd look for people going from zero to one, which means they are really using it, and for confidence rising, with people pushing boundaries and building.
