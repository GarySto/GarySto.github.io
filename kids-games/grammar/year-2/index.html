Year 2 Grammar and Punctuation

Year 2 adds commas in lists and the first apostrophes, plus four kinds of sentence: statements, questions, exclamations and commands. Children start joining ideas with words like because and when.

Below is everything on the Year 2 list in the national curriculum for England, in plain words with examples, plus the words your child will hear in class and a few easy ways to practise at home.

Punctuation in Year 2

Capitals, full stops, question marks and exclamation marks

Using them every time to show where a sentence begins and ends.

Commas in a list

Commas split up the things in a list. There's no comma before the final and.

  • We need eggs, flour, milk and sugar.

Apostrophes for missing letters

When two words squash together, the apostrophe goes where the letters are missing.

  • do not → don't
  • I am → I'm
  • it is → it's

Apostrophes for one owner

Add an apostrophe and s to show who something belongs to.

  • the girl's bike
  • Sam's coat

Sentences and words in Year 2

Four kinds of sentence

Statements tell, questions ask, exclamations show feeling and commands give orders.

  • The bus is late. (statement)
  • Is the bus late? (question)
  • What a long wait we had! (exclamation)
  • Wait at the stop. (command)

Noun phrases that add detail

Adding describing words to a noun makes writing clearer.

  • the blue butterfly
  • the man in the moon

Joining words

Join with or, and, but, and also when, if, that and because.

  • I wore my coat because it was cold.

Past and present tense

Keep to the same tense, including the -ing forms.

  • She is drumming. (present)
  • He was shouting. (past)

New words from endings

-ness and -er make nouns, -ful and -less make adjectives, -er and -est compare, and -ly makes adverbs.

  • happy → happiness
  • teach → teacher
  • help → helpful, helpless
  • slow → slowly

Compound words

Two words joined to make one.

  • whiteboard
  • football

Year 2 builds on everything from the years before, so children keep using it all. See Year 1 for what came just before.

Words your child will hear in Year 2

The curriculum lists these terms for children to understand and use in Year 2.

nounA naming word for a person, place or thing: teacher, park, ball.
noun phraseA noun with words that add detail: the big red ball.
statementA sentence that tells you something: The bus is late.
questionA sentence that asks something: Is the bus late?
exclamationA sentence that shows strong feeling. In Year 2 these start with What or How: What a long wait!
commandA sentence that tells someone to do something: Wait at the stop.
compoundA word made by joining two words: football, whiteboard.
suffixLetters added to the end of a word: help + ful = helpful.
adjectiveA describing word for a noun: a tall tree.
adverbA word that tells you more about a verb, often how or when: she ran quickly.
verbA doing or being word: run, think, is.
tenseShows when something happens: past (I walked) or present (I walk).
apostropheThe ' mark that shows missing letters (don't) or who owns something (Sam's coat).
commaThe , mark that separates things in a list or parts of a sentence.

Easy ways to practise at home

  • Write a shopping list as a sentence together and add the commas.
  • Spot apostrophes on food packets and decide: missing letters, or who owns it?
  • Take turns giving commands, then turn each one into a question.
  • Play Punctuation Archery (Full stops and capitals, or Commas and apostrophes): pull back the bow and shoot the balloon that finishes the sentence. Get it wrong and someone throws a tomato at you.
