How to Build a Marketing Dashboard People Actually Read

17 November 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

A marketing dashboard is only useful if the people it's built for actually open it and understand it. With AI, Power BI or plain HTML, anyone can spin one up in an afternoon now, so building it is no longer the hard part. The hard part is deciding what goes on it.

Why I made mine look like a game

My own dashboard for my AI teams is a small village, with the teams shown as people walking between buildings. I made it look like a game because it's more fun and more entertaining. So much of this world is boring text, tables and dashboards, and I wanted something I'd enjoy looking at.

I also wanted it to work for my 8-year-old daughter, who fell in love with it straight away. If an 8-year-old can glance at it and tell you what's going on, the adults reading it will have no trouble.

The first version took one evening

The first working version took a single evening. It was just buildings with little square boxes moving between them, and I still have the original pictures. From there I worked with Claude, a step at a time, to turn the boxes into something that looked like people. I wrote up the full build in how to build a live village dashboard for your AI agents.

I also shared the code as an open-source Village Starter, and it's been lovely to see other people pick it up. TotalWOR took it and adapted it into their own Guildhall Village, where every finished task lays a floor on the hall of its trade, and they credited me and the guide on their own credits page. Seeing someone build on it, and make it their own, is exactly why I gave it away.

Lots of friends messaged me saying it was great and fun, and asking how I'd done it. Someone also pointed out that villages like this seem to be a trend at the moment, which I was a little gutted about, but you can't always be a first mover on everything.

The questions to answer before you build

Because the building is quick now, the real work is in the questions. Before I'd build any marketing dashboard, I'd want answers to these:

  1. What information do the readers actually want? Start from their questions, not from the data you happen to have.
  2. Numbers only, or context too? Traditional dashboards can't always carry the words that explain a number, and sometimes the explanation is the part people need.
  3. Graphs, charts or tables? Pick the one that answers the question fastest for that reader.
  4. Which comparison? Year on year, month on month or week on week.
  5. Against what? Your closest competitors, or the market as a whole.

A dashboard that answers those five well will get read, whatever it looks like. One that skips them won't, however clever it looks.

Make it readable for everyone

I have type 1 diabetes and diabetic retinopathy, and many people have seen me wear an eye patch on business calls when my eyesight goes. It's who I am, and I'm not ashamed of it. Whoever your readers are, some of them will find small, pale text hard work. Big text, strong contrast and space between items help everyone, and they cost nothing to build in from the start.

Where I'd start

Write down the three questions your audience asks you most often, and build a dashboard that answers only those. Show it to one of the people it's for, ask whether it answers their question, and change it from there. You can add everything else later.
