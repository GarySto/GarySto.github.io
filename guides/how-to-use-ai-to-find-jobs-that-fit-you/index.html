How to Use AI to Find Jobs That Actually Fit You: Free Prompts for Every Level

Guide · Last reviewed October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

Tested on Claude, Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini in October 2026. ChatGPT not yet tested. AI apps change often, so let me know if one stops working for you.

A lot of people I know have been made redundant from big tech firms this year, and I keep seeing posts on LinkedIn and Reddit from people asking the same thing: where do I even start? Job boards show you hundreds of roles, and most of them aren't right for you. You end up spending hours reading adverts, and the few roles that do fit get lost in the pile.

I built a system for my own job search that flips this round. Before looking at a single advert, I decide what a good role looks like for me, write it down as a scoring rubric, and then get AI to search for roles and score each one against it. That way I only spend time on the roles that score well. This guide gives you the same approach as three free prompts, so you can start at whatever level suits you.

If you want to know why I built this and why it's free, I wrote about that in why I built a free AI job finder.

Level 1: StarterOne prompt, one conversation. About 20 minutes. Level 2: IntermediateSave your rubric and search again every week. Level 3: AdvancedA weekly shortlist that arrives on its own, in your AI app or free on GitHub.

Why a rubric beats scrolling job boards

A rubric is a simple scorecard. You list the things that matter to you, such as the type of work, salary, location and the skills a role asks for, and give each one a number of points. Every role then gets a score out of 100.

Mine has five sections: the work itself, the type of role, whether I can honestly meet the requirements, level and location, and whether the role will give me energy and help me grow. Anything scoring 75 or more, I apply for. Anything between 60 and 74 is a stretch I look at more closely. Anything below 60, I skip without feeling guilty. You will build your own in Level 1, and it will look different from mine, which is the point.

The scorecard also keeps you honest on bad days. When you've had a rejection, it's tempting to apply for anything. The rubric reminds you what you actually want.

Before you start

  • An AI assistant with web search switched on. Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini all work. Free plans are fine for Level 1, although you may hit daily limits. Check that web search is on before you start: if the AI can't search, it can't find live roles.
  • Your CV, with your personal details removed. Save a copy without your phone number, email address and home address. The AI doesn't need them to judge which roles fit. Your town or the area you can travel to is enough.
  • About 20 minutes for the first run, because the AI will ask you questions.
A word of warning: AI can get things wrong. It sometimes links to adverts that have closed, or misreads a salary. Always open the original advert and check it before you apply. When I tested these prompts, ordinary web searches turned up adverts that had closed more than a year earlier, so the prompts tell the AI to put the current month and year in its searches, check each advert's date and only list roles it actually found, with the link. Checking is quick, but do it.

Level 1: Starter prompt

This is one prompt that does everything in a single conversation. It interviews you, builds your rubric, reads your CV, searches for roles and scores them.

  1. Open your AI assistant and start a new chat.
  2. Click Copy on the box below and paste the prompt into the chat.
  3. Attach your CV (with personal details removed) or paste it in, then press send.
  4. Answer its questions. Short answers are fine. If you're unsure, say so and it will suggest options.
  5. When it shows you your rubric, check it and correct anything that's wrong before it starts searching.
Today's date is [type today's date].

You are my job search coach. Help me find roles that genuinely fit me, using a scoring rubric we build together. Work through these four stages in order and don't skip ahead.

Before anything else, check whether you can search the web in this chat. If you can't, tell me straight away, before the interview, so I can switch search on or use a different app.

STAGE 1: INTERVIEW ME
Ask me these questions a few at a time, not all at once. Keep it conversational. If I'm unsure, suggest options based on my CV.
1. What job titles or types of work am I interested in? What would I love to spend my days doing?
2. What work do I want to avoid?
3. What's my minimum salary, and what would I ideally like?
4. Where am I based, how far will I travel, and how many office days a week am I happy with? Am I open to fully remote?
5. What level am I aiming for (for example, the same level, a step up, or happy to step down for the right role)?
6. Which industries or types of company appeal to me, and are there any I won't work for?
7. What are my deal-breakers?
8. What gives me energy at work, and what drains me?

STAGE 2: BUILD MY RUBRIC
Turn my answers into a scoring rubric out of 100 points, with 4 to 6 sections. Weight each section by how much it matters to me. For each section, describe what earns full points and what earns low points. Include one section on whether I can honestly meet the role's requirements, based on my CV.
Set these thresholds: 75+ = apply, 60-74 = stretch (worth a closer look), below 60 = skip.
List my deal-breakers separately. Any role that hits a deal-breaker scores 0, whatever else it offers.
Show me the rubric and ask me to confirm or change it before you continue.

STAGE 3: READ MY CV
Read my CV and summarise in 5 bullet points my strongest selling points for the roles I want. Then list any gaps a hiring manager might worry about, and how I could address each one.

STAGE 4: SEARCH AND SCORE
Use web search now to find roles that are currently open and match my rubric. Search job boards, company career pages and LinkedIn job listings. Aim for 10 to 15 roles. Do the full search and scoring in one go, rather than stopping to show me a partial list.
Rules:
- Put the current month and year in your searches. Old adverts stay online for months, so this matters.
- Only include roles you actually found in your search, with a working link to the advert. Never invent or guess a role.
- Open each advert and check when it was posted or when it closes. Leave out anything posted more than 30 days ago or already closed. If you can't see a date, you can include it, but write "Date not shown, check" next to it.
- If you can't find enough good matches, say so honestly rather than padding the list.
- If the salary isn't shown, give half marks for the salary section and write "Salary not shown".
- If a recruitment agency posted the advert and doesn't name the employer, write "Agency: [name], employer not named" in the Company column, and don't give marks for the employer's brand.
- Link to the original advert where you can (the company's careers page or LinkedIn), rather than a site that copies adverts.
- Be strict. 75+ should mean a role I'd genuinely be excited to apply for, so usually only a handful will score that high.
Show the results as a table sorted by score, highest first, with these columns: Score | Verdict (Apply / Stretch / Skip) | Job title | Company | Location and working pattern | Salary (if shown) | Date posted | Link | One-line reason for the score.
Under the table, for my top 3 roles, tell me which parts of my CV to bring to the front when I apply.

Finally, give me my rubric again in one block of text so I can save it and reuse it next time.

Save your rubric. At the end, the AI gives you your rubric in one block. Copy it into a document and save it next to your CV. You will need it for Levels 2 and 3, and it saves you doing the interview again.

Level 2: Make it reusable

Once you have a rubric, you don't need to repeat the interview. The idea at this level is to save your rubric and CV in one place, so each week you can type one short message and get a fresh, scored list.

Most AI assistants have a way to save instructions and files that every new chat can use:

  • Claude: create a Project, add the instructions below as the project instructions, and upload your rubric and CV as project files.
  • ChatGPT: create a Project and do the same, or build a custom GPT if your plan includes it.
  • Gemini: create a Gem and paste the instructions in.
  • Copilot: if you have Microsoft 365 Copilot through work or a personal plan, you can build an agent with the same instructions. Use your own personal account for your job search, not your employer's.

If your assistant doesn't have any of these, keep the instructions, rubric and CV in one document and paste them in at the start of each chat. It works just as well.

Step 1: Paste these as the instructions

You are my job search assistant. My scoring rubric and my CV are attached (files called "rubric" and "CV").

Every time I ask you to search:
1. Search the web for roles that are currently open and match my rubric. Search job boards, company career pages and LinkedIn job listings.
2. Only include roles you actually found, with a working link. Never invent a role. Put the current month and year in your searches, open each advert and check its date. Leave out anything posted more than 14 days ago or already closed, unless I say otherwise. If you can't see a date, write "Date not shown, check".
3. Score every role against my rubric, out of 100. Any role that hits a deal-breaker scores 0. Be strict: only roles I'd genuinely be excited about should reach 75+. If the salary isn't shown, give half marks for salary and say so. If an agency posted it without naming the employer, write "Agency: [name], employer not named" and give no marks for the employer's brand.
4. Show a table sorted by score: Score | Verdict (Apply 75+ / Stretch 60-74 / Skip below 60) | Job title | Company | Location and working pattern | Salary | Date posted | Link | One-line reason.
5. Skip any role I have told you I've already seen or applied for.
6. For any role scoring 75+, tell me which 2 or 3 parts of my CV to bring to the front.

If I tell you a score feels wrong, ask me what's off and suggest a change to the rubric. Only change the rubric when I agree.

Step 2: Each week, start a new chat in your project and send this

Today's date is [type today's date]. Run this week's search. Roles I've already seen or applied for: [paste the job titles and companies, or say "none"].
Anything different this week: [for example "also look at contract roles", or "nothing"].

Step 3: Keep a simple tracker. Copy each week's table into a spreadsheet with an extra column for what you did (applied, saved, skipped). Paste the list of roles you've seen into Step 2 each week, so you only get new ones.

Tune it as you go. After a couple of weeks you'll notice patterns, such as roles scoring high that you'd never apply for. Tell the AI and let it suggest a rubric change. My own rubric is on its second version, and the changes made a real difference to what came back.

Level 3: Put it on a schedule

At this level the search runs every week without you, and you get a shortlist of new roles only. This is close to how my own system works: a weekly task searches, scores and adds new roles to my tracker, and I only look at the ones worth my time. There are two ways to do it. Route A needs no code. Route B is free on GitHub and you control everything.

Route A: a scheduled task in your AI app

You will need an AI assistant that can run scheduled tasks. Some paid plans of Claude and ChatGPT offer them. Look for an option called scheduled tasks, or Tasks, in your assistant's menu or settings. The names and plans change often, so check your own app.

  1. Set up Level 2 first, so your rubric and CV are saved in a project.
  2. Create a new scheduled task and set it to run once a week. Monday morning works well, because you start the week with a fresh list.
  3. Paste the prompt below as the task's instructions.
  4. If your assistant can connect to your files, such as Google Drive or OneDrive, give it access to one tracker spreadsheet only. If it can't, the task will send you the table and you can copy it into your tracker.
Weekly job sweep. Use my rubric and CV from my job search project.

1. Read my tracker spreadsheet (if you can access it) to see every role I've already seen. If you can't access it, use the list at the bottom of this message.
2. Search the web for roles posted in the last 7 days that match my rubric. Search job boards, company career pages and LinkedIn job listings, and put the current month and year in your searches.
3. Only include roles you actually found, with a working link. Never invent a role. Open each advert and check its date: leave out anything older than 7 days or already closed. Leave out any role already in my tracker.
4. Score each new role out of 100 against my rubric. Any role that hits a deal-breaker scores 0. Be strict. If the salary isn't shown, give half marks for salary and say so. If an agency posted it without naming the employer, say so and give no marks for the employer's brand.
5. If you can edit my tracker, add each new role as a new row: Date found | Score | Verdict | Job title | Company | Location | Salary | Link | Reason | Status (leave as "New"). Never delete or change existing rows.
6. Send me a short summary: how many new roles you found, then a table of only the roles scoring 60 or more, highest first. If nothing scored 60 or more, say so in one line. That's useful to know too.

Roles I've already seen (only needed if you can't read my tracker): [list]

Route B: the free GitHub version

I've also packaged the whole thing as a free GitHub template: github.com/GarySto/ai-job-finder. You make your own private copy, add your rubric and CV, and every Monday morning a GitHub Action searches for new roles, scores them, adds them to a tracker file and emails you the shortlist. It skips anything you've already seen.

  1. Open the repository and click the green Use this template button, then Create a new repository. Choose Private, because your CV will live in it.
  2. Paste your rubric into my-search/rubric.md and your CV, without contact details, into my-search/cv.md.
  3. Create an API key in the Anthropic Console and set a monthly spending limit.
  4. In your repository, go to Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions and add the key as a secret called ANTHROPIC_API_KEY.
  5. Click the Actions tab, then Weekly job search → Run workflow to test it.

The README walks through every click. GitHub Actions is free for this. You pay Anthropic a small amount per run for the AI and its web searches, and the template caps the searches so a run can't run away with your money.

Keep it safe. Only give a scheduled task access to the one tracker file it needs, never your whole drive or inbox. Never put passwords or job board logins into a prompt. And keep your job search on your personal accounts, not your work ones.

Getting better results

  • Be specific about location. "London, hybrid, two days a week maximum" works far better than "UK".
  • Name companies you admire. Ask the AI to check their careers pages directly. Company sites often list roles before job boards do.
  • Ask for adjacent titles. The same job is called different things at different companies. Ask the AI to suggest five alternative job titles for what you want, then add them to your rubric.
  • Challenge the scores. If a score looks wrong, ask the AI to explain it section by section. You'll often spot a rubric section that needs tightening.
  • Use the gaps. The CV gaps from Stage 3 are often the questions you'll get in interviews. Prepare answers for them.

Troubleshooting

  • It does the interview, then says it can't search. This happened to me in Gemini: it built a great rubric, then said it couldn't run a live web search. The prompt now asks the AI to check this before the interview starts. If it happens, reply "Please use web search now to find live roles", or copy your finished rubric into another app and carry on there.
  • Almost everything scores 75+. In my Copilot test, 12 of 13 roles came back as "Apply", including adverts from recruitment agencies that didn't name the employer. The prompts now tell the AI to be strict, give half marks when the salary isn't shown and not to credit an employer brand it can't see. If it still happens, reply "Re-score these more strictly. Only roles I'd be genuinely excited about should be 75+."
  • The AI says it can't browse, or the roles look made up. Web search is off or not available on your plan. Switch it on, or try a different assistant. Never apply to a role you can't open from the link.
  • The links go to closed adverts. Ask it to only include roles where it can see a posted date in the last 14 days, and to search company career pages as well as job boards.
  • Everything scores 70-something. Your rubric is too vague. Ask the AI to make the full-points and low-points descriptions much more specific for each section.
  • It keeps showing the same roles. Paste your "already seen" list each time, or use the tracker in Level 3.
