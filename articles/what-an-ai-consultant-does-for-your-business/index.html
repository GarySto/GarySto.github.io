What an AI consultant does for your business.

16 September 2026 · Gary Stow, Senior Client Solutions Manager, Financial Services, Microsoft Advertising / Microsoft AI

Every time a company talks about AI, the first thing that comes to mind is a shiny new technology or a buzzword‑filled presentation. In reality, most organisations need more than just a tool – they need someone who can translate data science into business value. That’s where an AI consultant steps in. They act as a bridge between technical teams and decision makers, helping you decide what problems to solve, how to model them, and how to roll out solutions that actually deliver results.

Defining the role of an AI consultant An AI consultant is not a single job title but a set of responsibilities that span strategy, design, implementation and governance. They start by listening to stakeholders to understand business objectives, then map those goals onto data assets and analytical capabilities. From there they advise on model selection, data architecture, and the trade‑offs between speed and accuracy. Finally, they work with product owners and engineers to embed the solution into production pipelines, monitor performance, and iterate based on feedback.

When you should bring in an AI consultant You’ll benefit most from an external AI consultant when your organisation has a clear problem but lacks the internal expertise to solve it. Typical scenarios include: - A data‑rich business that wants to move beyond descriptive analytics into predictive or prescriptive insights. - An existing data science team that needs guidance on scaling models, managing model drift, or integrating with cloud services. - A company exploring AI for the first time and needing a roadmap that balances short‑term wins with long‑term capability building.

In these cases an AI consultant can fast‑track adoption by providing a structured approach that reduces risk and accelerates return on investment.

Key skills and expertise you can expect A competent AI consultant brings together technical depth, business acumen and change‑management know‑how. They should be comfortable with statistical modelling, machine learning frameworks, and cloud platforms such as Azure or AWS. Equally important is the ability to translate complex findings into clear recommendations that resonate with executives and product teams. Strong communication skills, a collaborative mindset and an ethical perspective on data usage are also essential, especially when dealing with sensitive customer information.

Common misconceptions about AI consulting Many people think hiring an AI consultant means buying a pre‑built solution or that the consultant will write all the code for you. In practice, the consultant’s role is advisory – they help you choose the right tools, design experiments and set up governance processes. Another myth is that AI projects are quick wins; most successful initiatives require iterative experimentation, continuous monitoring and cultural change. Finally, some believe AI consulting is only for large enterprises – small and medium businesses can also benefit by focusing on high‑impact use cases such as customer segmentation or churn prediction.

What I’d tell someone starting from scratch If you’re looking to bring an AI consultant into your organisation, start by defining a clear business objective and identifying the data that will feed it. Then evaluate potential consultants based on their track record in similar domains, their ability to communicate with both technical and non‑technical stakeholders, and their commitment to ethical AI practices. Remember that the consultant is a partner – you’ll need to invest time, resources and organisational alignment for the partnership to succeed.
