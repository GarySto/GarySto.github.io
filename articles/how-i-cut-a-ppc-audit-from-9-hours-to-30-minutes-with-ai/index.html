How I Cut a PPC Audit From 9 Hours to 30 Minutes With AI

7 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Auditing every element of a PPC account used to take me nine hours or more. With an AI-assisted process it now takes under thirty minutes from start to finish, and it is even quicker if the data comes in through an API or an MCP connection instead of downloads. The point was never just speed, though, it was better recommendations, and this is how the process works in plain words.

Step One: Export the Right Data

The system can only be as good as what you give it, so the first job is pulling the key reports out of the platform. I export:

  • search queries
  • campaign and keyword performance
  • landing pages
  • ad copy
  • account settings
  • tags
  • publisher URLs

That covers every element of the account, in a form the AI can work through in one go.

Step Two: Set the Goal and the Rules

An audit without a goal is just a list of observations, so before the AI looks at anything I tell it what the account is trying to achieve and what rules apply. Real examples are things like generic not brand, a CPA of less than £20, growing share, or working within a limited budget. Change the goal and the recommendations change with it, which is exactly what should happen.

Step Three: Let It Find What a Tired Human Misses

With the data and the rules in place, the system works through everything and comes back with recommendations: poor performing keywords, wasted spend, places where budget should move, and performance challenges or opportunities. Every recommendation is tied back to facts and evidence in the data rather than gut feel.

This is where AI earns its place. After hours inside one account, a human gets tired and starts to miss things, and it gets hard to see the wood for the trees. The system does not get fatigued, and it does not bring the same bias you might bring to a campaign you built yourself.

Step Four: Check Everything Before You Act

None of this should be done blindly. Search queries, landing pages and ad copy still need a human review, and just because the AI says something is a great idea does not mean it is. I have seen AI outputs make things up with total confidence or quietly ignore the rules they were given.

So I finish every audit with an independent check. I ask the AI, in the same chat or a brand new one, to audit its own output against the facts, the data and what I originally asked for. That single habit has improved the quality of what I get back more than anything else.

What This Changes for PPC Teams

When I started in PPC, a team of ten people could spend their days on manual work like this. Today a team of two can do it, as long as they have the experience to judge the recommendations. The AI does the heavy lifting, and the human decides what is right for the client and why.
