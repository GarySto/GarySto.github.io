Year 4 Grammar and Punctuation

Year 4 is the big punctuation year: full speech punctuation, apostrophes for more than one owner, and commas after fronted adverbials. It's also where Standard English comes in, like we were rather than we was.

My eldest is in Year 4, so this is the page we use most at home.

Below is everything on the Year 4 list in the national curriculum for England, in plain words with examples, plus the words your child will hear in class and a few easy ways to practise at home.

Punctuation in Year 4

Full speech punctuation

A comma after the words that say who is speaking, and the end punctuation inside the speech marks.

  • The conductor shouted, “Sit down!” (the curriculum's example)
  • “Where are you going?” asked Mum.
  • “I love painting,” said Amara.

Apostrophes for more than one owner

One owner: add 's. Lots of owners ending in s: the apostrophe goes after the s.

  • the girl's name (one girl)
  • the girls' names (lots of girls)
  • the children's playground

Commas after fronted adverbials

When a sentence starts with when, where or how words, put a comma after them.

  • Later that day, I heard the bad news. (the curriculum's example)

Sentences and words in Year 4

Plural or possessive?

An s on the end can mean more than one, or it can belong to someone.

  • The dogs barked. (more than one dog)
  • The dog's bowl was empty. (belongs to the dog)

Standard English verbs

Using the verb forms that are correct in written English.

  • we were (not we was)
  • I did (not I done)

Longer noun phrases

Add adjectives, nouns and phrases to say more about a noun.

  • the strict maths teacher with curly hair (the curriculum's example)

Fronted adverbials

Start a sentence with words that say when, where or how.

  • Later that day, …
  • As fast as she could, …

Paragraphs around a theme

Each paragraph is about one idea.

Pronoun or noun?

Choose between a pronoun and a noun so the writing is clear and doesn't repeat itself.

  • Mia found a kitten. She took it home.

Year 4 builds on everything from the years before, so children keep using it all. See Year 3 for what came just before.

Words your child will hear in Year 4

The curriculum lists these terms for children to understand and use in Year 4.

determinerA word before a noun that says which one or how many: the, a, some, those, my.
pronounA word used instead of a noun: she, it, they.
possessive pronounA pronoun that shows who owns something: mine, yours, hers, ours.
adverbialA word or phrase that works like an adverb, saying when, where or how: Later that day.

Easy ways to practise at home

  • Write a short conversation between two toys, with speech marks and said, asked or shouted.
  • Find a plural possessive in a book (the twins' room, the players' boots).
  • Take turns starting a sentence with a fronted adverbial, like In the morning, … or Suddenly, …
  • Play Punctuation Archery (Mixed challenge): pull back the bow and shoot the balloon that finishes the sentence. Get it wrong and someone throws a tomato at you.
