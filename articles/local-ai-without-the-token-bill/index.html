No, you don't need to spend thousands a month to actually use AI.

12 September 2026 · Gary Stow

I keep seeing the same complaint doing the rounds on LinkedIn at the minute: token burn is out of control, the costs keep climbing, and nobody can actually justify the return on it. I get why people are saying it, I've seen the dashboards people are sharing, and for a lot of businesses throwing every request at the most expensive model available, it's genuinely true. But I don't think it's the whole story, and I can actually show my working rather than just assert it.

Everything I've built at home, GarAI, the trading system, the manager, supervisor and departments watching it, Wade running underneath all of it, has been done with no formal training, no computer science degree, and no ongoing budget beyond a Claude Pro subscription I was already paying for anyway. I'm not saying that to boast, I'm self-taught and still learning plenty of it as I go. I'm saying it because if I can build something this involved without spending thousands a month, the token burn complaint isn't really about AI being unaffordable. It's about how it's being used.

How the split actually works, in practice

The bit that makes the real difference isn't which model is cheapest per token, it's deciding honestly which requests need a paid model at all. In my own system that's not a vague principle, it's an actual three-tier setup the automation runs through every time something needs looking at.

The first tier auto-resolves anything that matches a confirmed, expected pattern, no model involved at all. The second tier, the one that actually saves the money, routes routine, bounded questions (checking whether something's broken, reading logs, writing up a plain diagnosis) to a free local model running on my own PC through LM Studio, no API call, no cost. Only the third tier, genuinely complex judgement calls, things involving real strategy, performance thresholds or anything urgent, goes anywhere near a paid model.

Getting that local tier to actually work took real, unglamorous engineering, not just switching a setting. The local model's context window was too small for the amount of trade and pipeline data I was feeding it alongside a file to review, so requests were failing outright. Fixed by widening the model's context and by stripping out anything it didn't need for that specific question, so it's not loading trading data to answer "what does this file do." None of that is exciting to write about, but it's the actual reason the free tier works reliably instead of just working in a demo.

The one thing I won't pretend away

I'll admit the one thing I can't wave away is the hardware. You do need a PC capable of running a local model reasonably well, and that's not free (I've written a step-by-step beginner's guide to building a local AI assistant covering exactly what to check first). I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But that's a one-off cost, not a monthly bill that climbs the more you use it, and if you've already got a half-decent PC sitting there, you've got most of what you actually need already.

So if you're a business or an individual looking at those token bills and wondering whether AI is actually worth it, I'd say the limiting factor isn't the technology anymore, it's whether you're willing to think about the problem differently.
