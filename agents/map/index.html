01

Why this map exists

The village shows the work. This shows the wiring.

My village draws my AI teams as a little town, with people carrying work between buildings. It is the easiest way I have found to explain what the system does, and my family love it. What it cannot show is a hand-off that has quietly stopped. A busy-looking village can still have a broken link in the middle of it.

In late October I ran a full check of everything, because new articles had stopped appearing and an idea I had submitted had disappeared. Every agent looked as if it was working. The problem was in the connections between them, and nothing was watching those.

Every line is checked, not just every agent

So I built a check that knows every agent and every hand-off, and asks of each one: did it run, did it produce something real, and did that reach the next agent? It runs every 30 minutes on my own PC. Anything that fails turns into a red line on this map and a job for my automation team to root-cause, rather than something I only notice a week later.

It also looks for agents left on their own. An agent with nothing coming in and nothing going out is doing work nobody uses, so it is flagged just like a break.

How it grew to 62

This started as one trading scanner. Every agent since has been added for a reason: each time a real problem came up, I built something to catch it permanently instead of fixing it by hand once. That rule is why the map keeps getting bigger, and why each new agent has to connect into the rest before it counts.

62 agents and 74 hand-offs running at the same time, from my home PC and GitHub, is the bit I am proudest of. It is far too many to hold in my head, which is exactly why it needed a map.

How to read it

The website team sits on one side of the sphere, the trading team on the other, and career and outreach underneath. My two goals glow at the centre, and every chain of agents should eventually lead to one of them. Click any light to see its job, what it receives and where it sends its work.

Some agents are marked private. They stay on the map so you can see they are connected, but what they do is kept to myself. The dashboards where I make decisions are shown simply as internal dashboards.
