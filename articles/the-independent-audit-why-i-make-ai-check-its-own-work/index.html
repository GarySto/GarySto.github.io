The Independent Audit: Why I Make AI Check Its Own Work

8 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

Something I talk about almost everywhere I go now is what I call the independent audit. I have noticed a number of AI outputs are simply wrong, and it seems to be happening more often. They make things up with total confidence, ignore guardrails and instructions, or make bold claims that nothing in the data supports.

What the Independent Audit Is

At the end of every output I ask for an independent audit. Sometimes I do it in the same chat if that works, and sometimes I open a brand new chat so the checker has no attachment to the answer it is checking. Either way, I ask it to go back over the work and check:

  • the facts, and whether each one can be backed up
  • the accuracy of any numbers or claims
  • whether the output actually answers what I originally asked
  • whether it followed the rules and guardrails I set

The quality of the outputs has improved significantly since I started doing this. I still do not fully know why I am seeing an overall drop in quality that needs this more often, but I know the check works.

Why It Matters for Marketing Data

In PPC and analytics, a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer. If an AI tells you a keyword is wasting money, or that a campaign has suddenly improved, you might act on it. Teaching a system the difference between a true insight and an outlier is one of the hardest parts of building anything useful with marketing data, and checking its work is how you catch the times it gets that wrong.

I build the same idea into my own projects at home. The automation team that watches my demo trading account has departments that find issues and a supervisor whose job is to root-cause them before anything reaches me, so nothing gets acted on just because one part of the system said so.

When the AI Goes Round in Circles

Checking is only half of it. I have spent time chasing problems that did not exist because an AI hallucinated, or forgot the rules and instructions I had given it earlier in the chat. When that happens, sometimes you just need to take a breath, go for a walk and come back with a different approach, ask the question slightly differently, or start a brand new chat. A fresh chat with the key facts often gets further than an hour of arguing with an old one.

Treat It Like a Graduate

The way I explain AI to people is that it is like a graduate fresh out of university, with loads of great ideas and suggestions but no real world experience. You would not send a graduate's first report to a client without reading it. The independent audit is just the AI version of reading it first.
