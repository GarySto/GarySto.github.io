How to Learn AI as a Marketer With No Coding Background

8 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

Written by Gary Stow with help from AI tools. Every fact is checked and every article is approved by Gary before it goes live.

I tried to learn Python a few years ago. I was looking at a move into an analytics and insights role at the time, and it was pointed out to me that Python was a skill I would need and a gap I had. If I am honest, I struggled to keep focus, and with my ADHD I got distracted and moved on to something else.

What Changed

What changed was having a reason. I had an idea about the stock market and wanted to build my own tool to find the opportunities I should be looking at, which meant learning GitHub and Python to make it happen. Learning for the sake of learning never stuck, but learning because the next part of my project needed it did.

AI was a different story. I was lucky to get access to AI tools early through work, and the more I explored them the more I realised the value this was going to have on the future of productivity in business. Then it just clicked and it caught my fascination. I started building agents to speed up my own daily tasks, and before long I had become the go-to person for requests and ideas.

Learning by Building

At home I kept pushing myself to learn more. I still have not taken any exams or certifications, and I am not an engineer, but I am building as much hands-on experience as I can. I built projects to test myself: can I automate a website, can I automate my stocks and shares trading, and can I learn and get better by doing rather than just reading.

That is the approach I would recommend to any marketer starting today. Start with a problem you actually want to solve, not a course. Start small and build up, and do not try to get it perfect straight away, just get it working with guardrails in place. Document every checkpoint, protect yourself against crashes and mistakes, and keep older files you can fall back on or use as a reference.

You Do Not Need a Technical Background

I often feel like the idiot in the room when people start using big words. I prefer the direct, simple approach, and that has turned out to be an advantage when learning AI, because the tools now do a lot of the technical heavy lifting if you can explain clearly what you want.

The mistakes are part of it too. I have spent hours, even weeks, building systems that were over complex, or that were made redundant overnight when new tech came out. I would not say any of that was wasted. Every one of them taught me something new or showed me something I needed to consider.

If you want to see where this approach has taken me, I wrote about how a colleague joked and dared me to build something, and how I went on to build my own local AI assistant using only free tools.
