Will AI Replace PPC Managers? Not the Good Ones

6 October 2026 · Gary Stow, performance marketing and AI

I get asked whether AI will replace PPC managers all the time, and my honest answer is no, although it is already replacing a lot of the work. When I started in PPC, a team of ten people could spend their days doing things by hand. Today that same work can be done by a team of two, and if you are in paid search that should make you sit up a little.

What AI Already Does Well in PPC

PPC has always been about data, causation and correlation, running tests and working out that if we do X then Y happens. That is exactly the kind of work AI is good at. Give an LLM the right context and a clear prompt and it can spot poor performing keywords, find wasted spend, suggest where budget should move, and flag performance problems and opportunities you might have missed.

Account audits are the clearest example I have. A full audit used to take me nine hours or more, and with an AI-assisted process it now takes under thirty minutes from start to finish, quicker still if the data comes in through an API or an MCP connection rather than downloads. I export the key reports and the system works through all of them:

  • search queries
  • performance
  • landing pages
  • ad copy
  • settings and tags
  • publisher URLs

It then makes recommendations against the goal and the rules I set for that account, such as generic not brand, a CPA under £20, or growing share on a limited budget.

The time saving is the headline, but the bigger win is that it takes bias and fatigue out of the job. After hours inside one account it gets very hard to see the wood for the trees, whereas the system does not get tired, and every recommendation comes back tied to the data that supports it.

Where It Falls Down

None of that means you can trust it blindly. Search queries, landing pages and ad copy all still need a human to review them, and just because an AI says an ad is great copy does not mean a person will think so.

I have also started to notice that some AI outputs are simply wrong. They make things up with total confidence, ignore the guardrails and instructions you gave them, or make bold claims nobody can back up. So at the end of every piece of work I now ask for an independent audit, sometimes in the same chat and sometimes in a brand new one, checking the facts, the accuracy and whether the output actually answers what I originally asked. The quality of what I get back has improved a lot since I started doing that.

The Skills That Matter More Now

The way I explain AI to people is that it is like a graduate fresh out of university, with loads of great ideas and suggestions but no real world experience. You have that experience, and your job is to use it to guide their ideas towards your actual problem.

That is why I do not think AI replaces PPC managers. You still need someone who can spot when things are not going right, who understands the human side of a client and the things that never get said or written down. You still need someone who knows what to do when a system breaks, or when the tokens run out because nobody paid the bill. Manual safeguards still matter, and so does enough knowledge to read a recommendation and decide whether it is good or bad, and why.

If I had to name the one skill that matters most now, it is the why: spotting the flaw in what the AI has given you, knowing how to fix it, and knowing enough to train the model in the right way. Those are the people who will run the team of two.
